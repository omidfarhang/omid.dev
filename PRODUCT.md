## Users

Primary: developers and Linux-curious practitioners from junior to senior — including frontend architects, engineering leads, and people working in Angular / TypeScript platforms, systems-minded frontend, DevOps, and related craft. The site should help Omid be known in those communities and improve the chance of good professional opportunities, without becoming a hiring funnel.

Also: readers who follow the same person across English, Persian, and German; and Omid himself, who uses the site as a personal knowledge base to return to.

**Jobs to be done**

1. Get to know Omid as a real person — interests, judgment, and work — not a corporate brand.

## Problem

Most technical blogs either chase novelty without systems depth, or bury useful craft in noise: generic SaaS chrome, tag soup, and posts that do not connect into a reading journey. Personal sites often over-narrow into a niche brand or over-sell the author. Readers looking for honest perspective, durable craft, and runnable companions have to piece that together themselves.

## Product purpose

omid.dev is Omid Farhang’s public home for evergreen and timely writing, short notes, resume/about surfaces, a Playground catalog of companion demos, and curl-installable Tools. It is a reflection of the person — interests, experiences, ideas, work, and personality — not a publication confined to one niche.

Most existing content is software engineering and technology. Topics outside that (health, electronics, everyday life, personal reflection, and anything else of genuine interest) are in scope when they are authentic and meaningful. Privacy is the hard limit: do not reveal overly private, sensitive, or identifying details about Omid or others.

**Success looks like**

- Content that stays findable (search, tags, series, reading paths).
- Return visits — including Omid using the site as a KB.
- Follows and other real interactions (email, messages) without sales pressure.
- Multilingual parity that respects RTL (`fa`); translations that read as original fluent writing, not English calques.
- Companions and tools that stay linked from the writing that announces them.
- A calm personal site that still feels like one person’s brand.

**Not optimizing for:** selling products, courses, or other commerce; engagement bait; SEO theater that constrains what gets written.

## Brand personality

Mono editorial, exact, and humane. Curious, thoughtful, technically capable, and interested in how things work — with a perspective of their own, hands-on exploration, and honesty about what is still being figured out. The site should feel like a real person, not a corporate brand or a content machine.

Professional identity as a frontend architect and engineering lead matters, but it is only one part of who Omid is. Competence, curiosity, and personality should show through the work — not through repeated self-assertion.

### Tone

Authentic, precise, understated. Prefer concrete trade-offs over slogans. Technical terms stay precise; metaphors stay short. No mandatory catchphrases. Adapt tone to subject and context.

**Avoid:** “thought leader,” “full-stack ninja,” “rockstar,” “guru,” “10x,” forced humor, artificial enthusiasm, corporate marketing language, unnecessary self-promotion, exaggerated expertise, or manufactured authority.

### Author framing

- Default identity: **Omid Farhang**.
- Use **Frontend Architect & Engineering Lead** when the subject warrants it (professional/technical writing, résumé-related surfaces).
- Short bios only where they add context — not on every page, not as a corporate author profile.
- Same person across `en` / `fa` / `de`. Never invent credentials, biography, clients, availability, rates, or endorsements.

### Languages

Same underlying personality in all three: thoughtful, direct, technically precise, approachable, authentic. Adapt to each language’s conventions; do not translate English phrasing literally. Translations must read as if originally written by a fluent speaker of that language.

| Language | Guidance |
|----------|----------|
| **English** | Baseline. Clear, conversational, precise; first person is fine. |
| **Persian (`fa`)** | Natural contemporary Persian; conversational but thoughtful. Avoid bureaucratic formality, stiff translation, and unnecessary English where Persian is natural. Keep English technical terms when they are clearer or conventional. |
| **German (`de`)** | Natural, idiomatic, friendly, direct. Prefer contemporary standard German over academic or corporate stiffness. Keep technical precision without unnecessary formality. |

Formality follows subject and audience, not language alone. Professional or sensitive topics may be more formal; personal notes may be more relaxed. Preserve opinions, personality, and intent across languages.

## Anti-references

- Generated output MUST NOT use purple-on-white / purple-to-indigo SaaS themes, warm-cream serif “editorial AI” skins, or broadsheet newspaper layouts.
- MUST NOT introduce a second accent family, blue-tinted neutrals, tinted gradients, glows, or washed surfaces — ink is the accent; color is only small marks (highlighter, category dots).
- MUST NOT nest boxes (cards in panels, footer columns as cards) or turn the first viewport into a dashboard of stats, pills, and promo chips.
- MUST NOT treat this site as a product marketing landing page, course funnel, agency template, or sales engine.
- MUST NOT invent one-off visual systems that ignore `DESIGN.md` tokens and primitives.

## Surfaces

The site is not organized around a single content type. Prominence should follow actual content and visitor needs.

| Surface | Role |
|---------|------|
| **Posts** | Core — substantial writing: technical articles, tutorials, reflections, developed ideas. |
| **Notes** | Core supporting — short observations, discoveries, references, standalone thoughts. |
| **Tools & Playground** | Supporting — utilities, interactive experiments, prototypes; discoverable without competing with writing. |
| **About & Resume** | Essential identity — About beyond the job title; Resume for professional background. |
| **Reading paths & series** | Editorial connections — learning progressions and related journeys. |

Do not force every idea into a predefined category. Preserve the distinction between personal identity, published writing, and interactive work without making them feel like disconnected sites.

**Format choice:** use the smallest format that serves the idea.

- **Long post** — needs explanation, context, evidence, examples, or a durable reference.
- **Note** — concise observation or reference that stands alone.
- **Tool** — practical, repeatable utility.
- **Playground** — interaction/experimentation is central to understanding.

Don’t inflate notes into articles, articles into interactive experiences, or every technical idea into a tool — and don’t default to prose when interaction would communicate better.

**Reading paths & series:** Agents may propose new paths, series, groupings, and cross-links with a clear rationale (progression, shared theme, useful collection). Do not create structure for page views or artificial navigation. Drafts and recommendations are fine; new curated paths, prominent nav changes, and substantial reorganizations need review before publication. Keep suggestions distinct from established editorial groupings until approved.

### Agent truthfulness

Agents must not fabricate factual claims about Omid (history, achievements, clients, qualifications, projects, endorsements, availability, commercial commitments) or invent personal experiences, opinions, quotations, or autobiographical stories as if they were real.

Agents may propose topics, creative directions, page concepts, and ways to express personality; they may draw clearly grounded conclusions from existing content. Do not turn assumptions into asserted facts. When information is missing: use general wording, omit unsupported specifics, or ask. Distinguish proposed content from verified autobiography; get approval before publishing new personal claims, commitments, or sensitive information.

Preserve freedom to express opinions, explore unfamiliar subjects, change one’s mind, and present a self beyond professional identity.

## Ship bar

Changes must preserve identity, accessibility, technical integrity, and multilingual support. Scope of verification is proportional to the change.

**Non-negotiable**

- Responsive layouts (mobile and desktop; no horizontal overflow or broken content).
- RTL and multilingual correctness (`fa` direction, alignment, punctuation, mixed script, component behavior; `en`/`de` remain correct).
- Light and dark themes with appropriate contrast.
- Visual consistency with existing tokens, type, components, and patterns — no new accent family or parallel design system without justified need.
- Accessibility: semantic HTML, keyboard use, visible focus, contrast, accessible names.
- Content integrity: front matter, URLs, metadata, links, code, formatting, language conventions; no drive-by edits to unrelated content.
- Technical quality: run relevant checks for the change (build, validation, etc.); do not claim checks passed unless they were run.

Prefer incremental changes that fit the established system. Substantial visual or architectural departures need clear rationale and review. Do not sacrifice usability, accessibility, or consistency for novelty.
